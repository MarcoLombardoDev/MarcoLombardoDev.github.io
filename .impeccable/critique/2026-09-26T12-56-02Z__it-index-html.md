## Design Specificity Verdict

The vertical red bars quoting the owner's own LinkedIn banner, the conic-spotlight portrait ring, the myth-named tool roster with a documented rationale, unhyped copy ("la risposta, con le prove, è nessuna" on Tyche), and 10 project cards that all deep-link to something real (live apps, live repos, a live client site, a live shop) read as authored for this specific person's actual inventory. The "Rare Red Rule" from DESIGN.md is genuinely followed: exactly two elements carry a resting glow (primary CTA, portrait ring) across the whole page.

**Deterministic scan:** `impeccable detect --json` against `it/index.html`, `en/index.html`, `assets/css/style.css` exited 2 with 169 findings (51 advisory, 118 warning). The overwhelming majority of the warning-severity count — 72 "low-contrast" findings — collapses to **1 real issue** on cross-check: the static scanner cross-multiplies every declared color against every other declared color in the stylesheet without respecting `@media print` scoping, so 70 of 72 are phantom pairings (e.g. `#000 on #0a0a0c`) from the print-only rule at `style.css:1382-1385`, never visible to an actual visitor. Re-running the same detector logic *in the live rendered browser* (which does respect media scoping) found exactly 1 low-contrast instance: `--muted` text over the single brightest point of the hero's radial gradient (3.88:1, fails 4.5:1 body-text AA, passes 3:1 large-text AA) — narrow and needs a manual look, not a systemic failure. The two named DESIGN.md tokens the task asked about, `--muted` and `--dim`, both pass AA comfortably against every real background in use (5.1:1–8.2:1).

Several other detector warnings are the system's own documented, deliberate choices, not defects: `dark-glow` (×2), `gradient-text`, `pulsing-dot` (×2), `hero-eyebrow-chip`/`numbered-section-labels`/`kicker-above-heading`, and `overused-font` (Inter) are all named and intended in DESIGN.md's "Rare Red," "One Voice," and kicker-as-second-typographic-voice rules.

What the detector caught that the design review missed, and that does look like a genuine defect: **`undersized-ui-text` ×20** — the project-badge chips ("Materiale", "Startup", "Tool", "Sito") render at 10.5px, under a reasonable legibility floor, on both languages. ## Overall Impression

The system-level discipline is real and holds up under both an opinionated design review and a mechanical scan — the palette, shape, and shadow rules are followed with unusual consistency for a portfolio site. The gut-check failure is localized: the 10-card Projects section (exactly where the hero's one CTA sends every visitor) flattens four genuinely different categories — 2 startups, 6 open-source tools, 1 client site, 1 shop — into one undifferentiated scroll, and the site's only conversion path (the `mailto:` contact button) has no fallback if it silently fails. Both are fixable without touching the visual language at all.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The Projects grid gives 10 cards across 4 real categories no visual grouping.** PRODUCT.md itself organizes these as 2 startups, 6 open-source tools, 1 client site, 1 shop — but the homepage flattens all 10 into one undifferentiated 2-column scroll with only a small text badge ("Tool"/"Startup") as differentiator. The 6 tool cards additionally share an identical icon and badge (confirmed by the design review) and an identical 52×52 icon-tile shape pattern the detector independently flags as generic (`icon-tile-stack`). A visitor who came for the two startups has to read through 6 look-alike cards to be sure they've seen everything, and this is precisely the section the hero's one CTA points to.
**Fix:** Add a small subheading/visual break before the tool cluster ("Strumenti open-source"/"Open-source tools"), or color/shape-differentiate the badge by category.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

**[P1] The site's only conversion action has no fallback if it fails.** Grepped both `index.html` files: the email address appears nowhere as visible or copyable text — only inside the `mailto:` href. Any visitor without a configured default mail client (common on managed devices, Chromebooks, webmail-only setups) clicks "Scrivimi una email"/"Send me an email" and hits a dead end with no way to recover the address short of view-source.
**Fix:** Print the address as visible text near the button, or add a copy-to-clipboard affordance.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

**[P2] The cookie notice covers the hero's primary CTA on first mobile load.** Confirmed via a 390×844 screenshot: on first paint, the fixed-bottom cookie bar covers roughly the bottom third of the viewport, pushing the H1's second line, the lead paragraph, and the "Guarda cosa faccio" CTA out of the initial view. A distracted mobile visitor's first screen is an eyebrow, half a headline, and a dismiss-only banner — not the one thing the hero is built to sell.
**Fix:** Auto-collapse to a small corner tab on mobile, or delay/shrink it so it never overlaps the fold's primary CTA.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

**[P2] Primary button label fails WCAG AA contrast by a narrow margin.** Computed contrast of the button's white text (`#f4f4f6`) on Signal Red (`#e8112d`) is 4.20:1; at 15px/700-weight it doesn't reach the "large text" size exemption (~18.7px bold needed), so it falls short of the 4.5:1 normal-text AA minimum — on the one element the whole system is built to make people notice.
**Fix:** Darken the red slightly for this specific fill, or bump the label size past the large-text threshold.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

**[P2] Chip/badge text renders under a legible floor.** The project-badge chips ("Materiale", "Startup", "Tool", "Sito") render at 10.5px on both languages — confirmed by the detector (`undersized-ui-text` ×20) independent of any brand-intent exception.
**Fix:** Raise to at least 11–12px; the pill padding has room.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable typeset`
